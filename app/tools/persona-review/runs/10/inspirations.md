# Sophie Dubois — Inspirations

A gathered set. Each line names what Omega should take, in this register, from the source.

## Houses (the floor of restraint)

- https://www.chanel.com — *Take: monochrome as discipline, not aesthetic. Black, white, one act per page.*
- https://www.hermes.com — *Take: identity above, utilities below. The name is given a row of its own before any control appears.*
- https://www.cos.com — *Take: a five-item navigation. The wordmark gets a full row; everything else recedes to a hairline.*
- https://www.lvmh.com — *Take: a custom serif voice that can carry stationery, a wordmark, and a portfolio gateway without changing register.*

## Cultural institutions (the register Omega must inherit)

- https://www.fondationcartier.com — *Take: the custom typeface. Every exhibition is the same institution speaking. Omega is a venue, not a screen.*
- https://area17.com/clients/fondation-cartier — *Take: the case study. Note how a single colour per show, drawn from the printed poster, lets the digital surface stay achromatic.*
- https://fontsinuse.com/typefaces/147516/fcartier — *Take: the proof point — institutional bodies commission their type, they do not borrow it.*

## Editorial intelligence (the rhythm reference)

- https://www.kinfolk.com — *Take: whitespace as composition, not as backdrop. Pages start busier and are reduced until only the load-bearing element remains.*
- https://alexhunting.studio/blogs/projects/kinfolk — *Take: the Kinfolk redesign. Custom type, mixed serif/sans at matched x-height, no widows, no rivers, no ugly hyphenation.*
- https://klim.co.nz/in-use/kinfolk-magazine/ — *Take: the type-pairing rule — the serif and sans share dimensions so the page never feels collaged.*
- https://a-magazine.be — *Take: the discipline of one curator, one thesis, one issue. Omega's brand voice should change less than its data does.*

## Technique notes (used in the critique)

- https://dirtylinestudio.com/luxury-brand-typography/ — *Lesson cited: Chanel's bold sans wordmark in the 1940s — minimal, modern, assertive — is still load-bearing eighty years later.*
- https://www.chroitor.com/brand-design-lessons-from-chanel-crafting-a-legacy-of-luxury/ — *Lesson cited: powerful without shouting.*
- https://premiumcoding.com/the-new-language-of-luxury-website-design-how-digital-experiences-shape-desire-in-2025/ — *Lesson cited: the user is "guided, not rushed." Motion is measured. Restraint is the medium.*
- https://productiontype.com/portfolio/lvmh — *Lesson cited: a "future classic" type system — high contrast, airy spacing, sharp details — applied across stationery, signage, wordmark.*

## Anti-references (the cost framing)

- https://www.binance.com — *The bazaar. Trustpilot and Coin Bureau both note users describe the platform as "overloaded with too many tabs and buttons," "scary for beginners," "cluttered with features." The cost: institutional desks reviewing Omega cannot encounter the same visual register as the venue they are trying to avoid.*
- https://coinbureau.com/review/binance-us-review — *Confirmation: the cluttered-interface critique is not a stylistic preference, it is a usability finding. Read this before adding any campaign banner anywhere on Omega.*
- https://www.trustpilot.com/review/binance.com — *Confirmation: customer reviews on the same theme. Banners and tabs proliferating is what users name when they describe what they don't trust.*
- https://www.okx.com — *Campaign-heavy. Hero carousels, promotional CTAs in the chrome. Omega's chrome carries the wordmark and nothing else.*
- https://coinmarketcap.com — *The ranking-list aesthetic. Omega is not a leaderboard. The /batches page is the closest Omega comes to a list view, and even that is set as an attestation log, not a ranking.*
- https://opensea.io — *Marketplace genericness — the tile-grid product-card pattern. Omega has no tiles. It has a venue, a desk, and a record.*

## What we do not take

- Not Playfair, not Bodoni, not Crimson, not Lora — the brand brief rejects all of them. We use Source Serif 4, italic-only, display-only — and we use it on more pages than we currently do.
- Not gradients. Not coloured fills under chart areas. Not saturated colour at rest on segmented controls.
- Not exclamation points, not banter, not a single emoji glyph anywhere in committed work.
