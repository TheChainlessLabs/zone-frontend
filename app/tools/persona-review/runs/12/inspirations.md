## Trading layout

- https://help.dydx.trade/en/articles/166979-interface-of-the-trade-tab
  What we take from it: keep chart, ticket, and post-submit management in one locked workstation, because traders should not re-orient after every action.
- https://docs.gmx.io/docs/trading/order-types/
  What we take from it: execution details belong in the trading path, not buried in a separate explainer after the click.
- https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/order-types
  What we take from it: market versus limit has to feel different in the hands, not just in the label.
- https://docs.drift.trade/protocol/trading/order-types
  What we take from it: market orders need slippage awareness and limit orders need visible control logic.
- https://docs.drift.trade/protocol/trading/order-types/advanced-order-types
  What we take from it: once traders rest size, they expect flags and trigger mechanics nearby, not hidden in another route.
- https://support.backpack.exchange/exchange-1/account-functions/order-types-and-executions
  What we take from it: post-only, IOC, and self-trade prevention are not edge cases for power users; they are ticket posture.
- https://help.coinbase.com/en/coinbase/trading-and-funding/advanced-trade/dashboard-overview
  What we take from it: open orders and order panel adjacency is table stakes, even if the rest of the venue is not my taste.

## Private execution

- https://renegade.fi/
  What we take from it: midpoint, privacy, and no public book should be explained as the product, not whispered as a disclaimer.
- https://www.coinperps.com/learn/dark-pools-in-crypto-explained
  What we take from it: institutions come for stealth, slippage control, and MEV protection, so the interface should surface those benefits bluntly.
- https://legalclarity.org/how-do-dark-pools-work/
  What we take from it: midpoint price improvement and execution uncertainty are the trade-off pair; show both.

## Unified account context

- https://www.aave.org/help/supplying/supply-tokens
  What we take from it: dashboard-first flows are useful for capital management, but they are the wrong primary mental model for a live trading screen.
- https://help.coinbase.com/en-gb/coinbase/trading-and-funding/advanced-trade/what-is-advanced-trade
  What we take from it: bundling too many adjacent product promises around trading dilutes urgency.

## Anti-references

- https://wise.com/us/send-money/
  What we take from it: transfer calm kills trader urgency; do not let the ticket read like remittance.
- https://vercel.com/docs/dashboard-features/overview
  What we take from it: spacious dashboard chrome is fine for projects and logs, not for an execution venue that needs faster scan loops.
