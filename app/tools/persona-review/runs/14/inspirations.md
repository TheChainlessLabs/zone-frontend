# Hana Mori — inspirations

External pages consulted while reviewing Omega. One line each, in this persona's register.

## Atmospheric restraint (the floor)

- https://www.aesop.com/us/r/philosophy-to-design/ — what we take: maximum effect with minimum material; a room that lowers its ceiling to make you exhale.
- https://www.wallpaper.com/gallery/lifestyle/a-visual-history-of-aesops-best-designer-stores — what we take: restraint as a sequence of bespoke responses to a setting, never one template repeated.
- https://design-anthology.com/story/aesop-by-design — what we take: the brand is the air between objects, not the objects.
- https://www.dezeen.com/2016/11/21/norm-architects-minimalist-workspace-office-gallery-interior-design-kinfolk-magazine-copenhagen-denmark/ — what we take: zones of progressively increasing privacy; a dark pool product surface should be sequenced the same way.
- https://normcph.com/project/kinfolk-gallery/ — what we take: tonal plaster (Kabe), oak, no chrome — replicate the *tonal* logic in dark-mode greys, not the materials.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Praise_of_Shadows — what we take: dark mode read by gradients between blacks, not by highlights against black.

## Typographic + editorial register

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallpaper_(magazine) — what we take: 7-column grid where most columns are deliberately empty above the fold.
- https://lettersfromsweden.se/acnestudios/ — what we take: type cut for a single brand at specific optical sizes; nothing generic in the chrome.
- https://composuremagazine.com/the-silent-luxury-revolution-how-the-row-redefined-modern-elegance/ — what we take: white space *is* the layout. No promotional banners, no merchandising.
- https://www.typza.com/blog/10-minimalist-luxury-websites — what we take: the inventory of luxury websites where typography is architectural, not decorative.
- https://www.dezeen.com/2016/10/11/welcome-to-new-look-dezeen-update-refresh/ — what we take: when an editorial site has too much character it strips back to one austere structural mark and one accent hue.

## Material + colour discipline

- https://www.finnishdesignshop.com/en-us/manufacturer/muuto — what we take: powdery atmospheric tones; colour is climate, never emphasis.
- https://www.muuto.com/ — what we take: object photography against tonally adjacent backgrounds, never high-contrast cutouts.

## Anti-references (cited with a behaviour cost)

- https://www.coindesk.com/business/2021/11/17/ftx-cryptocom-and-the-stadium-curse — what we take: stadium-brand vocabulary (pulsing dots, high-saturation chips) reads, to an institutional buyer, as the brand that's about to fold. Behaviour cost: a desk screening Omega will read promotional UX as compliance risk.
- https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8680-23 — what we take: the regulator's case against Binance singled out aggressive marketing-and-VIP-routing as the failure pattern. The visual register of a product trades on the same signal.
- https://ui.aceternity.com/components/grid-and-dot-backgrounds — what we take: the canonical "blueprint grid" import is now the SaaS-template tell. If a sophisticated buyer recognises the component library on first paint, the atmosphere is over.
- https://www.setproduct.com/blog/complete-guide-to-blueprint-grid-design — what we take: the Vercel grid trend has saturated; "if the grid is the first thing you notice on the page, it's too strong."

## Institutional vocabulary (the floor for the *product*)

- https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/products/trading/trading-venues/btbu/ — what we take: an institutional execution surface is silent about itself and loud only about its data.
- https://www.bloomberg.com/professional/insights/trading/innovation-driving-change-in-dark-pools-yet-unintended-effects/ — what we take: dark-pool products communicate by what they suppress, not what they showcase.
